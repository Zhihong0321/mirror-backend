01

01 · The blind spot

Owners cannot see the version
of themselves that buyers see.

Inside the business

The story the owner tells

  • — The website they wrote and approved.
  • — The Facebook page they curate.
  • — The pitch they rehearse on every call.
  • — The licence they renewed last year.

All of it true. Almost none of it what a stranger meets first.

The internet's version

What a buyer actually meets

  • — A directory still printing the phone number changed in 2021.
  • — A Maps listing filed under the old legal name.
  • — A professional registration two days from expiry.
  • — A 2019 award the owner never displayed.
  • — Sixty staff reviews nobody has read.
  • — An AI assistant that names six competitors and not them.

The gap between those two columns is the product. No agency will audit a $500k company for $500 an hour. That is the entire market.

02 · Why now

Four things changed at once.
The gap became sellable.

01

Buyers ask AI first

“Who should I hire for this?” now returns a short list. If the internet describes you wrongly, or not at all, you are not on it — and you never hear about it.

02

Public records went free

RDAP, certificate transparency, DNS, PageSpeed, Wayback, open professional registers. Evidence that used to need a researcher now needs an HTTP call.

03

Inference costs cents

A twelve-lane, evidence-first audit now costs less than a coffee. The same work billed by a consultant is $500–5,000 and takes weeks.

04

The long tail is unserved

Eleven million SMEs across four launch markets. Agencies serve the top one percent. The rest have never had an external audit of any kind.

AI made writing cheap. It did not make evidence cheap. Evidence is the moat — and it is the part everyone else skips.

The bait

04 · How it works — the free path

Five steps. One job each.

Every step reads the previous step's saved file. A step never does another step's job, so any step can be re-run, tested or replaced without touching the rest.

01 · Research

Gather, never judge

Official-site gate. Own pages first, then country searches, then the 122-check catalog. Maps, LinkedIn and Naver read by our own worker PCs.

step1_research.json

02 · Technical audit

Deterministic, zero LLM

PageSpeed mobile + desktop. Domain expiry and transfer lock. SPF/DMARC spoofability, TLS. DNS, certificate logs, Wayback, YouTube, Maps reviews.

assets row

03 · People

Who is tied to the company

Public people and contact drift: the decision maker, a dead phone field, ex-staff still listed. Company data only — no private numbers.

people row

04 · Analyse

The only judge

One LLM call and one recheck produce four lists: findings, contradictions, suggestions, notes. Namesakes dropped. Not found is not a finding.

step2_*.json

05 · Write

Never judges

One call writes the summary, the email and the proof line. Plain code checks every sentence against the Step 2 ids, then renders the page itself.

page + email

Why the split matters. Judgement is the only expensive, unreliable part. It happens once, in one place, against saved evidence — so quality is measurable and cost is bounded.

No kill gates. A step that fails is recorded and labelled “not run”. The report is still produced, and the gap is named rather than hidden.

Sources: docs/v21.md, docs/v21-step2.md, src/v21/ (steps 1, 1.5, 1.6, 2, 3). Outputs are the actual stored file and row names.

07 · The advantage

Any model can write an audit.
Almost none can gather one.

The model is a commodity and we treat it that way: the cheapest capable model, called once for judgement and once for writing. What cannot be bought off the shelf is the check catalog, the evidence discipline and the reader fleet.

A competitor can copy the page in a weekend. Copying 122 researched checks across four legal systems takes a research team a year — and then the next country starts.

01

The catalog is data, not code

122 checks in 21 country × industry packs. Each names its source, the rule behind it, the exact query and a manual fallback. A new industry is a research task; a new country is a layer, not a rewrite.

02

Discipline is enforced in code

Namesakes dropped. Own-site claims filtered out of “the internet says”. Not found is never a finding. Every claim carries a quote and a URL, and the writer is checked sentence by sentence against the ids it cited.

03

Reads run on our own machines

Pages, PDFs, Maps, LinkedIn and search results are fetched by worker PCs we already own. Paid indexes are used only where there is no free path. That is why the marginal cost is cents, not dollars.

04

One dataset, two products

Preview and report read the same rows, so the paid report can never contradict the free one. The preview's count is what the free check found, never a promise about the total.

05

The outcome graph compounds

Every finding, rejection and register hit is stored. In a year that is a benchmark no competitor can buy: how a Johor contractor compares with 400 others, and which checks actually predict a lost tender.

14 · Risks

The five ways this fails.
And what is already in the code.

Risk Already in place What this round adds
Platform access.
LinkedIn and Google can block readers or change terms.		Own worker PCs, an independent search index, ordered fallbacks. No single source is required for a report to exist.A second reader path per source, plus a legal review of every data route.
Accuracy.
A confident wrong claim about a business is a liability.		Quote and URL only. “Cannot be confirmed” instead of “is false”. Namesakes dropped. Sentence-level checks before publishing.Spot-check queue, errors-and-omissions cover, a written correction policy.
Willingness to pay.
A $59 one-off is a thin wedge if nobody renews.		Free proof, a refund guarantee, and an agency channel where one buyer pays for many clients.Monitoring subscription and price tests across the four markets.
Commoditisation.
Anyone can wrap a model and call it an audit.		The value is in registers, directories, namesakes and people — not prose. That is a year of research per country.Published benchmarks that only our outcome data can produce.
Privacy.
PDPA and its equivalents govern personal data.		Company data only; no private mobile numbers; redaction rules enforced before a page is stored.A named data-protection owner and an annual audit.

What would actually kill it: if preview-to-paid conversion stays below two percent. That number is measured weekly, per market and per industry. The guarantee caps the buyer's risk; measuring conversion caps ours.
