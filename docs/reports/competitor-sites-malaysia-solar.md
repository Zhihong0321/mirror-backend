### Gaps and opportunities

- Offer a clearly labelled self-service bill estimate before requiring contact, then let visitors send their result to an engineer. A few competitors already have calculators, so the opportunity is to make the assumptions, limitations and next step especially clear.
- Explain indicative pricing or a transparent quote breakdown where commercially practical. Most supplied sites sell through tailored enquiries rather than published system prices; Solaroo's all-inclusive quote promise is a useful benchmark.
- Standardise proof with a date, definition and scope for each metric, and reconcile totals across pages. This would make project and installation claims easier to trust and compare.
- Put warranty terms, response times, exclusions and the maintenance process in a concise comparison-friendly format; several sites promise support, but the supplied summaries rarely show this level of detail.
- Create distinct homeowner, business and developer enquiry paths, each with relevant examples and financing explanations, rather than making every visitor interpret a broad corporate offer.
- Consider tested Malay and Chinese content or assistance for Malaysian prospects. Language selectors appear on some regional sites, but the supplied evidence does not establish a multilingual Malaysian benchmark.

### Own website vs competitors

Eternalgy is ahead on the supplied visual assessment of its homepage hero and is unusually specific about engineering accountability, named staff, maintenance activities, fault response and warranties. It is broadly level with established competitors in offering end-to-end delivery, financing, project proof, credentials and a direct enquiry route; its sitemap is extensive, although distinct page counts and content quality have not been verified. It is behind competitors such as SOLS Energy, Xurya and SUN Energy on visible self-service calculator access: its bill simulation requires a WhatsApp exchange. The most important improvements are to add an optional self-service estimate with clear assumptions, make the assessment and quote process easy to understand before contact, and keep dated project metrics consistent and clearly defined. The screenshot shows only Eternalgy's hero, so the visual quality and usability of the rest of its site cannot be ranked.

## Conclusion

Eternalgy already has a credible position built around engineering ownership and long-term accountability rather than generic solar savings alone. Its strongest opportunity is to pair that technical credibility with a more immediate, transparent customer journey, especially an optional self-service estimate and a clear explanation of what happens after enquiry. Consistent, dated proof and concise service and warranty terms would make its claims easier to assess. Any implementation priorities should be validated through testing, since the supplied material does not show conversion performance or the full usability of the sites.
