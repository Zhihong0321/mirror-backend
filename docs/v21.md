# Company Dive V2.1

## Why V2.1

V2 grew into one long pipeline where every stage researched, judged and wrote at once. It became hard to optimise and hard
to change. V2.1 makes one cut: **three steps, one job each.** Each step reads the previous step's saved file, so each step can
be improved, rerun and tested on its own.

| Step | Job | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | Gather. Find the official site, read it, search where the owner does not look. No judging. | `step1_research.json` |
| 2. Analyse | Judge. Find contradictions, outdated or unproven claims, missed advantages. Apply "not found is not a finding". | `step2_*.json` |
| 3. Write | Write the owner-facing health check from Step 2's findings only. | the report |

A step never does another step's job. When a step has a problem, fix it in that step.

## What the company dive is for

- The company dive is **not** the deeper dive. The deeper dive is the paid full report.
- The company dive runs **at a loss**. Its only job is to be good enough bait that the owner buys the report ($59).
- The owner knows their own business better than the internet does. A report that tells them what they already know is
  worthless to them.
- What the owner **does not** know is what the internet says about them in places they do not watch. **The bait is the gap
  between what the owner thinks customers see and what customers actually see.**

## Why Step 1 looks like an audit

Because the bait is that gap. Step 1 searches like an auditor, outside the company's own channels, for what an owner cannot
see from inside: a stale phone number in a directory, a licence near its end date, a legal name that differs between
sources, an award they won but do not show, staff reviews they never read.

The company's own site and social pages are only the **baseline**: what the owner says about themselves. Everything found
elsewhere is compared against it in Step 2.

Examples from the first five runs (2026-11-03), all from outside the company's own site:

- Dezeno: CIDB G7 registration ending 05/11/2026, two days after the run.
- YYC Advisors: the site footer names YYC Holdings Sdn Bhd; Facebook and VerifiedMalaysia name YYC Advisors Sdn Bhd.
- DNE Logistics: directories carry different phones and names, and namesake companies a customer could confuse with them.
- O2O Digital: LinkedIn lists it as a "Public Company"; its own blog title is dated "(2027)".
- Kingdom Digital: d Awards entries its site may not show.

## Step 1: the role

**Step 1 = coverage.** It is good when it searched in the right places and brought back the critical information. It does
not decide what anything means: no found / not found, no problem, no warning. That is Step 2.

### Gate: the official site

1. The given website must load and carry the company name. If not, try the best non-directory result from the search.
2. **No official site confirmed: skip the company.** Two reasons:
   - a business without its own site will not pay $59 for a report on its public information;
   - without the official site there is no baseline, and the report quality drops too far.

### Coverage: where to look

Each source must pass two tests:

1. **Can a search actually read it?** A register behind a form, a login or a paywall returns nothing. Drop it.
2. **Would the owner not already know it?** If they would, it is not bait. Drop it (or keep it only as baseline).

| Source | Readable | Owner does not know | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official site: home, about, contact (legal name, registration no., phone, address, claims) | yes | no | **baseline** |
| Own social pages: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn | yes | no | **baseline** |
| Directories and data sites: ZoomInfo, LeadIQ, contact.page, MyPages, VerifiedMalaysia, Crunchbase | yes | yes: old phones, addresses, names | **search** |
| Google Maps: pin, phone, hours, rating, unanswered reviews | needs a Places-type source | yes | **search** |
| Customer review and industry platforms: Atap, Qanvast, Recommend.my, GoodFirms, Clutch | yes | yes | **search** |
| Employee reviews: JobStreet, Glassdoor, Indeed, Hiredly | rating and snippets via search | yes | **search** |
| Award and member lists the company is on | yes | partly: missed advantages | **search** |
| News and press | yes | partly | **search** |
| Registers readable free (e.g. a published CIDB grade and end date) | sometimes | yes | **search, if readable** |
| Registers behind a form, login or paywall (CIDB CIMS, LAM, MyCukai, MySST, SSM) | no | — | **drop** |

The industry catalog adds industry sources (award lists, platforms, member lists) under the same two tests.

### What Step 1 saves

For each source: the query actually run and what came back (URLs, titles, snippets, page text). Raw, with no status or
verdict. The official site pages and the anchors read from them (legal name, registration number, phone, address).

### What Step 1 does not do

- No found / not found labels. A search returning nothing proves nothing, and saying so is Step 2's rule to apply.
- No dropping of namesakes, no contradictions, no wording for the owner.
- No code to work around forms and paywalls. A source that cannot be read is removed from the list.
